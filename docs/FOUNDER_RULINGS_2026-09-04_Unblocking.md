# Part Two: standing authority, so the run does not stall

## 7. Standing tiebreak
When a session reaches a decision that is genuinely close on merit, and it is not two-key, not member-facing, and not a semantics change to a shipped primitive, take the option that keeps existing meanings intact, open a row for the alternative, and report the call rather than asking. A close call is a report, not a question.

## 8. Migration splits are pre-authorized
Any item that turns out to need more than one migration splits into consecutive sessions on your own judgment, stated in the plan, without waiting for me. One migration per session still holds.

## 9. Queue-row corrections are pre-authorized
When a census contradicts a queue row, correct the row and record both the correction and the evidence, without asking. The original claim stays visible as wrong rather than being deleted.

## 10. Guard findings are rows, not questions
When a red proof surfaces a weak or mis-scoped guard, open a queue row with both-directions acceptance criteria and continue. Bring it to me only if fixing it would change a shipped behaviour or touch a two-key adoption.

## 11. Deploy authority for shadow and server-only work
You may run `tooling/deploy.sh` yourself, after preflight, when all three hold: the merge carries no member-facing change; every pending migration is additive; and preflight reports exactly what the plan predicted. Report the transcript rather than waiting for my word. My approval stays required for anything member-facing, anything non-additive, and any deploy where preflight reports something unexpected, including a surprising pending count.

## 12. Missing specs do not stop the run
When an item's spec is not in the repository, open the blocked row with the intake condition named and proceed to the next unblocked item.

# What is never pre-authorized
Anything member-facing. Anything that widens an allowlist, adds a guard excusal, or lowers a guard's floor. Anything touching a two-key adoption. Those cost a round trip every time, because they are exactly where a fast default goes wrong quietly.
