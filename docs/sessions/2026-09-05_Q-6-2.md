## The reserved decision, brought as two options

The founder reserved this one: **does a `decide` item land as a new
ledger state, or does it reuse `decision_record`?** Both are set out
below with the semantics change spelled out, per her instruction, and
the pre-committed tiebreak of 4 September applies if they are close.

### What `decision_record` means TODAY

One routed corporate choice: `question`, `recommendation`,
`alternatives`, `evidence`, an `authority_class` from A0 to A5, an
`audience` constrained by CHECK to `hom | corporate | founder`, a
`routed_by` who is a staff user, an optional expiry, and an outcome that
is whole or absent with its decider. Its own header says the client
audience is **deliberately absent until the client freeze lifts**, and
the single-consent rule is structural: one row is one choice with one
decider, and no batch path exists by design.

### Option 1: reuse `decision_record`, adding `member` to the audience

**What it costs, stated as the semantics change rather than as a
migration size.** The CHECK becomes four values, and from that moment
`decision_record` means two different things: a choice routed to a
member of staff under an authority class, and a choice routed to the
household. Every existing reader of the table (the drill-in, the
route/decide service, the expiry sweep, the payload guards) silently
acquires a second population it was not written for, and each would need
to be re-read to say whether it still means what it says. **The
authority-class vocabulary is the sharpest instance:** A0 to A5 are the
COMPANY's authority ladder, and a household deciding its own affairs is
not exercising a rung on it, so a member row would either carry a class
that does not apply to it or need a null that the CHECK currently
forbids.

**What it buys.** One table, one lifecycle, no duplication of the
outcome-is-whole discipline, and M-25 becomes a count over one relation.

### Option 2: a new ledger state on a new table

The Commitment Ledger is its own table, carrying the ledger's own
lifecycle and the Handled invariant's four clauses as columns, with
`decide` items as one state within it. `decision_record` is untouched
and keeps its one meaning.

**What it costs.** A second place where a choice can live, and a
mapping between them that a later session has to hold: an item routed to
the household in the ledger and a corporate choice in `decision_record`
are both "a decision" in English.

**What it buys.** The shipped primitive keeps its meaning; the Handled
invariant's four clauses get columns of their own rather than being
bolted onto a table whose outcome discipline was designed for a
different question; and the freeze split stays clean, because a
member-facing state that never ships is a state on a table nothing else
reads.

### My reading, and the call

**They are close on merit**, which is exactly the case the founder's
pre-committed tiebreak names. Option 1 is genuinely cheaper today and
its cost is entirely in the future; option 2 pays a mapping cost now to
avoid a semantics change later.

**So the tiebreak applies and the answer is OPTION 2, the new ledger
state.** Her reason, recorded rather than paraphrased: a shipped
primitive whose client audience value is deliberately absent is already
doing a job, and two meanings in one table is the cost that is hardest
to reverse. One thing I would add from reading the table: the
`authority_class` CHECK makes the second meaning visible rather than
merely conceptual, so option 1's cost is not hypothetical.

**Reported as a call, not asked as a question**, per the standing
tiebreak. Option 1 is recorded here so the alternative is legible if she
disagrees, and reversing to it later is a migration rather than a
rewrite.

## The plan

### The table

A Commitment Ledger row is one commitment the household's record has
made or is being asked to make, with the Handled invariant as columns:
an accountable owner, whether a required member decision is unresolved,
whether a follow-up or watch exists where external completion is
pending, and whether verification is satisfied or explicitly pending.

**`handled` is COMPUTED from those columns, never stored**, so no row
can claim to be handled while a clause is unmet. That is the invariant
made structural rather than rendered: the same posture as
`time_segment`'s computed duration, and for the same reason, which is
that a stored answer and its inputs drift.

### Routing

The Decision Rights block is the ROUTING TABLE, in the spec's own words.
Routing reads the household's `decision_right` rows and produces one of
three outcomes, and the **null-threshold case is built and proven
first** per the founder's instruction: a household with no matching
right, or a right with no ceiling, **routes to the member and never
auto-executes**. The safe direction is the one that asks.

### M-25

Decisions surfaced per household per week, **computed** from the ledger
by the same rule everywhere, never stored. The spec's supporting reads
(the auto-executed-to-asked ratio) are internal and stay corporate-side.

### Deliberately NOT in this session

- The member decision inbox as a surface, and any member delivery.
  Freeze-gated.
- Quiet hours enforcement on delivery, which binds when a member channel
  exists and today has none for this surface.
- Any change to `decision_record`.
- The stage-tag ROUTING onto `trigger_rule.stage`, which the founder
  ruled belongs to the WK-APP-002 conversion (Q-19).

## What was built

- **The Handled invariant is a CHECK**, not a rendering rule: a row cannot be
  closed while an accountable owner is missing, a required member decision is
  unresolved, an external completion is pending with no follow-up, or
  verification is neither satisfied nor explicitly pending. Adopted law that
  lives only in a service layer is a convention; here the database refuses.

## Two things reported rather than decided

**1. `approaching` is read as OPEN AND NOT YET HANDLED, not as "due soon".**
The queue row names four display states and they have to be a total function
over the ledger. The temporal reading needs a threshold for how near "soon"
is, which is barred, and it is also NOT total: an item with no owner and no
due date falls through all four. The chosen reading needs no knob and covers
every row, so it is the one that keeps existing meanings intact (the standing
tiebreak). If the founder wants the temporal reading it is a nullable `due_at`
plus a founder-set window, with nothing approaching while the window is unset.

**2. `blocked` exists in the routing vocabulary and nothing produces it.** The
never-decide list is prose in the values package, and turning a sentence into
a machine rule is a taxonomy. The vocabulary carries the value so the
founder's rules need no migration (the notification-firewall posture, where
five destinations shipped and the policy produced two).

## A guard that fired, and what was NOT done about it

`action-permissions.test.ts` refused all seven actions when the gate call sat
one hop away inside a shared `ledgerGate` helper. **The gate was inlined in all
seven rather than teaching the guard to follow a helper**, because that change
would widen what counts as gated for every action in the file, and widening a
guard to get a build through is exactly what the standing rule bars. Seven
repeated lines cost less than a guard that reasons about indirection.

Four other guards fired red first on the new table and were satisfied with real
treatments rather than allowlists: erasure coverage (blank to markers, keep the
skeleton and every timestamp, so all four CHECKs survive erasure), the archive
census (CORPORATE_ONLY with the freeze as the stated reason), the legal census
(a CHILD_DATA row), and the G-13 staff-disclosure guard (an allowlist entry
with a written reason, since the disclosure itself is founder-approved text
this session must not edit). **That entry is now a counsel-review candidate
rather than a next-revision one**, because G-13 was reclassified the same day
as a hiring precondition: accountable ownership says a named person is
answerable for an outcome, which is exactly what someone should read before
signing, and this is the third staff-attributed table to ship under that rule.
