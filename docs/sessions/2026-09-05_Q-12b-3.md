## The founder's reading, which settles the hardest part before it is built

Given 5 September 2026, and it removes the only question this row would
otherwise have to stop on:

1. **The five steps are the vocabulary AS WRITTEN**: `preferred`,
   `approved_substitute`, `established_backup`, `vetted_bench`, `ask`. Not
   renamed, not extended, not reordered.
2. **Which step is permitted without asking is READ FROM DECISION RIGHTS**,
   never minted here, on the every-ceiling-permits reading.
3. **If a per-household ordering is needed, that is a DECISION to report**,
   not something to build.

## What the five steps mean, and why the order is not mine to choose

The vocabulary is a LADDER: `preferred` is what the household wants,
`approved_substitute` and `established_backup` are things somebody already
approved for this household, `vetted_bench` is a company-vetted option nobody
has approved for this household in particular, and `ask` is the floor. **The
ladder's ORDER is given by the vocabulary itself** (the RFC lists it in that
order and the words describe descending closeness to the household's own
choice), so no ordering is invented by building it.

**What IS a per-household question, and is reported rather than built:**
whether a given household's ladder skips a rung. Two households could
reasonably differ on whether `vetted_bench` is reachable without asking at
all. Nothing in this build lets a household reorder or skip, and nothing
assumes they cannot; the column records which step a plan reached, and the
authority question is answered per attempt by the rights.

## The authority rule, applied rather than restated

**A step is permitted without asking only if the household's Decision Rights
permit it, under the standing rule that a grant in one domain never governs
another.** So the permission question is asked per FALLBACK PLAN against the
right its own row names, exactly as `expected_event` does, and the composition
where several rights share a materiality is the one already ruled: every
applicable ceiling must permit.

`ask` is the floor and needs no right: it is the step that means the authority
ran out.

## Shadow, and what this build deliberately cannot do

Year-two, `shadow`, on the same terms as its two siblings: computes, logs,
visible in the corporate portal, surfaces to no member and alters no HOM
briefing. **Nothing executes**, here as in Q-6: reaching a step means the
household's rights PERMIT it, and no execution engine exists to act on that.
