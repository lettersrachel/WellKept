## The blocking finding: Q-6's queue row says migration "none"

Read exactly from `docs/BUILD_QUEUE.md` line 47, the migration column
for Q-6: **`none`**.

**Q-6 cannot be built without new tables**, and the check is not a
judgment call. The schema census, run rather than recalled:

- No table carries a Commitment Ledger. `grep` for commitment / ledger /
  right / threshold across the `pgTable` set returns exactly two names,
  `pausedDecision` and `decisionRecord`, and neither is one.
- `decision_record` is ONE routed choice with a recommendation,
  alternatives, an authority class, an outcome and an expiry. It is a
  decision, not a ledger of commitments, and it carries no Handled
  state: no accountable owner field, no member-decision-unresolved
  state, no follow-up-exists state, no verification state. The four
  clauses of the Handled invariant have nowhere to live.
- **No table carries a spend ceiling of any kind.** The only money
  columns in the schema are `price_cents`, `cost_cents` and
  `amount_cents`, all records of what something COST, none a limit on
  what may be spent without asking. Decision Rights is a routing table
  keyed on exactly such limits.

So the row's `none` is wrong rather than restrictive, and building
around it would mean inventing a home for state the schema has no room
for.

### Q-6-1: the Decision Rights block (one migration)

The routing TABLE has to exist before anything can route on it, and it
is the half with a settled seed.

- Per-household Decision Rights entries: the right, its value, its
  materiality (the signed three-value enum), and provenance.
- **Seeded from the intaken values package**, per the authorization:
  `docs/intake/2026-09-04-founder-values/decision_rights_by_tier.csv`,
  17 rows across essential / family_operations / concierge, including
  `decisions_that_always_require_member` and the three spend rows.
- **Recorded as RECOMMENDATIONS until the COO and the founder confirm
  the rows**, which is a stored provenance state, not a comment.
- The plain-language mapping, authored corporate-side, with provenance.

**A design proposal rather than a question, because there is a clean
answer.** The standing rule keeps financial figures out of source, and
the capacity precedent lets reviewed operational figures in. Both are
satisfied without a ruling if the loader READS the tier defaults from
the frozen CSV already in the repository rather than restating them in
a TypeScript constant: the figures enter once, under the intake that
already froze and hash-pinned them, and no second copy exists to drift
from the first. If the founder would rather they be a source constant,
that is a one-line correction to this plan.

**Two adoption residues already on the record** (values-intake ground
truth 9), named here so the migration meets them deliberately: the
`decisions_that_always_require_member` row carries materiality `all`
and `authority_review_cadence` carries a blank, and **the signed
three-value enum can store neither as written**. They are not defects
in the enum; they are two rows whose materiality is a different kind of
statement. The mapping decides how they land, and I will report the
shape rather than widen the signed enum.

### Q-6-2: the Commitment Ledger, the Handled invariant, M-25, and the routing (one migration)

- The ledger, with the Handled invariant as SERVER-SIDE STATE: an
  accountable owner exists AND no unresolved required member decision
  AND a follow-up or watch exists where external completion is pending
  AND verification requirements are satisfied or explicitly pending.
  Every clause required; activity is never closure.
- **M-25 computed, never stored** (the derived-value precedent, and
  `latest_safe_start` is ruled derived-and-materialized by the same
  logic).
- **The routing clause moved off Q-5 by founder ruling**, in full:
  below-threshold auto-executes and logs completely; above-threshold
  lands as `decide`; never-decide blocks entirely; **and a null
  threshold sends everything to `decide` and executes nothing**. That
  last clause is the one worth building first and proving hardest,
  because it is the fail-closed direction and it is the state every
  household is in on day one.

**One design question I will not answer alone.** Where a `decide` item
lands is either a new ledger state or an existing `decision_record`
row. `decision_record` already carries the audience vocabulary with the
CLIENT audience "DELIBERATELY absent until the client freeze lifts",
which is the same freeze split this row is under, so reusing it is
attractive and would make the member-facing view a thin read exactly as
the authorization asks. It is also a semantics change to a shipped
primitive. I will report the two shapes with their consequences at the
Q-6-2 plan rather than pick one here.
