## 3. Ruling: time segments never tax the capture budget
The brief's segment taxonomy (access, setup, active, movement, dwell, waiting, remote, documentation, travel) is adopted as the STORAGE model, not as a field-capture obligation. The input spine's perfection standard stands unchanged: three interactions or fewer per observation, close-flow under two minutes. Therefore v1 segments derive automatically from what the spine already captures: arrival and departure taps, close-flow step timestamps, and travel inferred between visits. An optional refinement surface may let a HOM subdivide a visit after the fact; it is never mandatory, never in the critical path of close-flow, and its absence never blocks an estimate (the confidence model absorbs coarse segments as lower confidence, which is exactly what the estimate hierarchy is for). Manual per-segment timing in the field is prohibited in v1: precision that costs the HOM attention in a member's home is precision purchased at the wrong price.

## 4. Dependency: the canonical Task ID source
The brief's chain begins at the global Task Inventory (the 344-task catalog), and Task Inventory v1.3 is the one document the library reconciliation could not locate (standing punch item). WL Gate 0 is blocked on resolving it, founder-side, one of two ways: locate the original v1.3, or authorize reconstruction from the playbooks, the Standards Store, and the catalog's citation trail, registered as v1.4 with a lineage note. Until resolved, WL Gate 1 objects may build against a provisional task list seeded from the active playbooks and the HZ/HO records, flagged provisional in the schema so no evidence rows bind permanently to task IDs that may renumber.

## 5. External priors: the Source Registry and its walls
The brief's Source Registry is adopted with its usage-rights metadata mandatory on every row. Two walls, restated so they cannot blur: priors describe TASKS, equipment, and industry patterns (manufacturer service intervals, government maintenance clocks, published time-use research); a prior is NEVER data about a specific member household, and no external lookup about a member property enters through this door or any other (REQ-084 applies to the forecasting layer in full). Creator and press content in the registry is manual-curation reference only, per the brief's own guardrail: notes and citations, no ingestion, no reproduction, no commercialization of protected content. Manufacturer documentation and government sources are the preferred prior classes; the registry records source, scope, and rights for every coefficient that ever traces to an external origin, so any estimate can answer "why do you think that" all the way down.

## 6. Accuracy discipline (the shadow doctrine, applied to forecasts)
Forecasts are internal-only until they earn trust the same way triggers do: from WL Gate 3 onward, forecast-versus-actual error is tracked per household and reviewed in the weekly note alongside the shadow log scoring. No forecast drives an automated action; forecasts inform the founder's capacity decisions and the hiring-gate view, and the record of their accuracy is what will one day justify, or refuse, the optimizer.

## 7. Priorities unchanged
The founder's order stands: input spine and HOM Cockpit perfect first; this layer's Gate 1 rides that work rather than competing with it; primitives, engine, dashboards follow; client side untouched. The weekly note's "deliberately not built" line now also covers this layer, and the first entry is already known: the optimizer.
